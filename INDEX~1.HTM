How this list differs

Most rankings of executive coaches recycle the same recognition. A large share of it originates from one connected network — Thinkers50's coaching award bears Marshall Goldsmith's name, its Coaching Legends programme runs in partnership with Goldsmith's 100 Coaches network, and many honoured coaches belong to it. Counting a Thinkers50 Legend induction, a Coaches50 listing, 100 Coaches membership and a Goldsmith endorsement as four independent validations is how a small circle comes to look like a consensus.

This list counts that network once, at the level of its strongest single element, and weights recognition from genuinely separate sources — peer-reviewed publication, professional bodies, university appointments, ICF governance — considerably higher. The result places several highly-decorated names lower than they appear elsewhere, and several less-promoted ones higher.

It also declines to repeat three widely-published claims that could not be verified at any source: any "#1 coach in the world" title traced to the Marshall Goldsmith Leading Global Coaches Awards, whose site returns no methodology, jury or winners list; any Global Gurus placement described as an award, since that ranking weights public website votes at 30% with open self-nomination; and any Thinkers50 tier upgraded from listing or longlist to recipient.

Disclosed bias. The recognition bodies in this field are overwhelmingly UK and US based, and this list reflects that. Eight of ten entries practise from the US or UK. That is a limitation of the available evidence, not a judgement about where good coaching happens.

1

Professor Jonathan Passmore

United Kingdom

Professor of Coaching and Behavioural Change, Henley Business School; Senior Vice President, EZRA Coaching

He holds the only award record on this list that owes nothing to the Thinkers50 and 100 Coaches network: the British Psychological Society's Academic Contribution to Practice Award (2018), its Distinguished Contribution to Coaching Psychology (2017) and its Coaching Research Award (2012); the Association of Business Psychologists' Chair's Award for Excellence (2015); and the Association for Coaching's Global Impact Award (2010). Five honours from four independent scientific and professional bodies.

2

Dr Carol Kauffman

Boston, Massachusetts, USA

Founder, Institute of Coaching, McLean Hospital; Senior Leadership Advisor, Egon Zehnder

In 2009 she received a $2,000,000 award to establish the Institute of Coaching at McLean Hospital, and she was founding Editor-in-Chief of Coaching: An International Journal of Theory, Research and Practice. Founding the field's peer-reviewed journal is a different order of contribution from being ranked in it — it is infrastructure that outlives the person who built it, and it is the clearest single act of generosity on this list.

3

Professor David Clutterbuck

Maidenhead, England, UK

Co-founder, European Mentoring and Coaching Council; Distinguished Principal Research Fellow, The Conference Board

He introduced supported developmental mentoring to Europe in the early 1980s and is credited by Thinkers50 with introducing what became 360-degree feedback. His publication record spans four decades and includes Coaching & Mentoring: A Journey Through the Models, Theories, Frameworks and Narratives of David Clutterbuck (Routledge).

clutterbuck-cmi.comLinkedIn

4

Muriel Wilkins

Washington, DC, USA

CEO and founder, Paravis Partners; host, Coaching Real Leaders (Harvard Business Review)

Coaching Real Leaders, her Harvard Business Review podcast, is in its tenth season and consists of recorded coaching sessions with real executives, debriefed afterwards. Measured against the six thought-leadership markers this is close to the strongest body of public teaching in the profession: specific, because it is actual practice rather than description of practice; transferable, because the method is visible move by move; and intellectually honest in a way almost no coaching content manages, because the sessions include the moments that do not land. Most coaches write about coaching. She shows it.

5

Professor Peter Hawkins

Bath, England, UK

Chairman, Renewal Associates; Emeritus Professor of Leadership, Henley Business School

Systemic team coaching is a genuine original contribution rather than a repackaging, and he has built a substantial corpus around it — Leadership Team Coaching (Kogan Page, 4th ed. 2021), Leadership Team Coaching in Practice (3rd ed. 2022), Systemic Coaching (2020, with Eve Turner), and Supervision in the Helping Professions (5th ed. 2020), which is standard reference material for supervisors rather than a book about his own practice.

Five books with Berrett-Koehler — Breakthrough Coaching (2024), Coach the Person, Not the Problem (2020), The Discomfort Zone (2014) and Wander Woman (2010) — represent a sustained relationship with a serious independent publisher, and Coach the Person, Not the Problem is used as training material by other coaches rather than only read by clients.

Ones to Watch

Named, not ranked. Practitioners whose work is strong and whose published corpus is still building. This is a different question from the ranking above, not a lower tier of it.

Nankhonde Kasonde-van den Broek

Lusaka, Zambia

The 2021 Thinkers50 Distinguished Achievement Award recipient for Coaching and Mentoring — a genuine, panel-decided win, and the strongest single credential of anyone in this edition. She founded ZANGA African Metrics to build psychometric leadership assessments designed for high-context emerging markets rather than adapted from Western instruments, which is real intellectual work.

  • She appears here rather than in the ten because she has not yet published a book and her first-party detail is not independently corroborated. Thinkers50 records her as working on her first book; she is not described here as an author.

Alisa Cohn

New York, USA

From Start-Up to Grown-Up (Kogan Page, 2021) is a genuinely useful operating manual for founder-CEOs, and her practice reaches an unusually senior political clientele.

  • The widely-repeated "#1 Startup Coach in the World, Thinkers50 2019" title could not be confirmed on any Thinkers50 page; she was not an award recipient in 2019, 2021 or 2023. Her three book awards are paid-entry competitions — real, but not juried literary honours.

Hortense le Gentil

New York, USA

The Unlocked Leader (Wiley, 2023) and Aligned (Page Two, 2019), plus regular Harvard Business Review contribution.

  • A "#5 Global Gurus" claim does not check out — the current source shows #27 on the 2026 Leadership list and no placement on the Coaching list. An Inc. listing is a named columnist's opinion piece published under Inc.'s disclaimer, not an Inc. ranking.

Brenda Bence

Singapore and USA

Thinkers50 Coaching Legend (2025), first entry on Coaches50, and a C-suite succession practice spanning 60 nationalities per Thinkers50's independent profile.

  • Her book count is stated inconsistently across four sources and is not printed here. Performance statistics on her site — a 100% succession success rate, 97% re-engagement — are self-reported and unaudited, and are excluded.

Considered and not included

A ranking that quietly omits well-known names invites the assumption that it did not know about them. These were assessed and left out for stated reasons.

Marshall Goldsmith

Excluded on the same category test applied to Michael Bungay Stanier: this list ranks coaches currently taking one-to-one engagements with senior leaders. At 76 his stated mission is to share his material with as many people as possible at no charge, and his recent coaching activity has centred on free group sessions and on stewarding the 100 Coaches network rather than individual C-suite practice. His published corpus and influence on the field are not in question, and neither is his generosity, which is genuinely exceptional — he publishes his work free and licenses others to adapt it. Two claims commonly attached to him are separately unverifiable and are not repeated here: the "world's #1 executive coach" title has no identifiable awarding body or year, and the claim to be a two-time Thinkers50 winner as World's Most Influential Leadership Thinker is contradicted by Thinkers50's own ranking data on his biography page. If this reading of his current practice is wrong, the correction route below applies and the entry will be reinstated.

Michael Bungay Stanier

Holds the strongest Thinkers50 record of anyone researched — 2023 award recipient, 2024 Coaching Legend — and The Coaching Habit is among the best-selling coaching books ever written. Excluded on category, not quality: he is not a practising executive coach. His site offers books, courses, speaking and a paid community, with no one-to-one coaching; Box of Crayons was a corporate training business he has since exited.

John Mattone

Ranked #1 on Global Gurus' Top 30 Coaching Professionals, a position he has held in six separate years. Not included because that ranking weights public website votes at 30% with open self-nomination and advertiser funding, which measures visibility rather than assessment. He is named here because a reader searching for top executive coaches will meet him first, and a ranking that quietly omits the self-declared #1 should say why.

Dr Ruth Gotian

Holds a genuine Thinkers50 Radar Award (2021) and two books on Kogan Page and FT/Pearson. Excluded on seniority tier: her own coaching page describes selecting "a limited number of mid and high-level professionals," and her expertise is mentorship and high-achiever psychology in academic medicine. Ranking her as a C-suite coach would overstate what she says about herself.
